# India GST accounting scope

Specified for the user's selected jurisdiction, India. This is a scoped regular-registration accounting implementation, subject to independent Indian tax review before a stable release or live statutory use.

## Supported behavior

Configure one GST registration in INR books, its postal address, state name/code, three output liability accounts and three reviewed input-credit asset accounts. Standard control accounts can be created from **Company & periods**. Registration mappings are immutable; another registration uses separate books. The application checks GSTIN structure and state consistency, not the registry or GSTIN checksum.

An invoice can explicitly enable domestic regular forward-charge GST. Enter place-of-supply state/name, counterparty GSTIN when registered, counterparty/delivery addresses, and a reviewed rate and HSN/SAC on every line. Purchases require the supplier's invoice reference/GSTIN, place of supply matching the company's registered state, and explicit independent input-credit review. Rates are user inputs; there is no hardcoded product-rate catalogue or automated eligibility decision. Four-, six-, and eight-digit codes are structurally supported; the correct required classification/detail remains a reviewed input.

For ordinary intra-state supplies, central and state/UT components apply; ordinary inter-state supplies use integrated tax. The implemented branch follows supplier registration state versus the entered place of supply, consistent with the GST Portal's domestic invoice workflow. Place of supply is not inferred from a billing address. Special transactions can differ and are excluded here. [Official GST Portal invoice instructions](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Creation_of_Outward_Supplies_Return_in_GSTR-1.htm).

GST sales numbering is restricted to 1–16 alphanumeric, hyphen or slash characters; numbers remain globally unique in the books, which is stricter than annual reuse. Export includes registration/address information, date, number, line descriptions/quantities, HSN/SAC, reviewed rates, taxable values and separate tax components, place of supply, reverse-charge status and a supplier-authorization space. Review supplier authorization and all applicable legal particulars before issue. [CBIC Rule 46](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-rules/1000136/1000001), [CBIC place-of-supply circular](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/circular-cgst-90.pdf).

## Explicit nil-rated, exempt and non-GST supplies

For a whole sales or purchase document, select **Record India tax details**, choose nil-rated, exempt or non-GST, and record the independently reviewed classification basis. All line rates must be zero; the application does not infer exemption from a missing tax input or select a legal notification for the user. HSN/SAC, supplier/recipient particulars and the place of supply remain required. Mixed taxable/exempt lines use separate documents in this implementation; composition and zero-rated exports/SEZ are separate, unsupported regimes.

Sales exports produce a **Bill of supply** for nil-rated/exempt supplies or a **Non-GST invoice**, with the entered particulars, HSN/SAC, supply values, review basis and supplier-authorization space, without tax-rate or tax-charge columns. Review all applicable Rule 49 particulars independently. [CBIC Rule 49](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/14112019-CGST-Rules-2017-Part-A-Rules.pdf), [CBIC bill-of-supply guidance](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/sectoral-faq.html).

Classified purchases require the reviewed supplier state, matching the supplier GSTIN when one is entered, and a supplier document reference; unregistered suppliers can be recorded. No input credit is recognized, and the input-credit eligibility checkbox must be false. Expense/inventory and payable use the tax-free supply value. Linked quantity returns preserve the original classification and proportionate supply value; no tax-adjustment declaration is required for a classified zero-tax return.

GSTR-1 drafts aggregate classified outward values into the four inter/intra-state and registered/unregistered EXEMP groups, in separate nil/exempt/non-GST columns. GSTR-3B book worksheets separate outward nil/exempt values in 3.1(c), non-GST in 3.1(e), and classified inward values in table 5, split by reviewed supplier state. They do not classify composition purchases. Ordinary taxable values remain in 3.1(a). Internal `taxable_minor` fields on classified records carry the supply value; this is not a claim that exempt/non-GST supplies are taxable. [GST Portal table mapping](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR3B.htm), [GST Portal EXEMP instructions](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Creation_of_Outward_Supplies_Return_in_GSTR-1.htm).

The optional `gst.classification` has `kind` (`nil_rated`, `exempt`, `non_gst`), `reason`, and a purchase-only `supplier_state`. Omitting it preserves the original regular taxable behavior and retry payload. Classified records require schema/backup version 5; versions 1–4 remain readable. Restore re-derives classification-sensitive postings, notes and tax amounts and rejects unsupported classifications or a false older backup-version label.

## Exact calculations and postings

The rate is stored as integer basis points: entered 18.00% means 1,800, rather than binary floating point. The taxable extension is rounded half up to paise. Each intra-state component rounds half up independently at half the combined rate; integrated tax rounds at the full rate. This is an explicit invoice-accounting rounding policy. Statutory return rounding/adjustments need separate review; the app does not round a return liability for filing.

For a synthetic 1,000.00 taxable example with a reviewed combined rate of 18%, an intra-state sale debits receivables 1,180.00 and credits income 1,000.00, output CGST 90.00 and output SGST/UTGST 90.00. An inter-state example credits output IGST 180.00. The 18% fixture is an arithmetic example, not a product-rate recommendation.

An eligible-credit purchase at the same example amounts debits expense/inventory 1,000.00 and reviewed input tax 180.00, and credits payable 1,180.00. Recoverable GST does not inflate inventory carrying cost. Payments and outstanding invoices use the gross tax-inclusive amount. Tax profiles, entered particulars, calculated amounts and linked journals are checked again during backup restore.

Input credit is not established just by receiving an invoice. Its availability can be restricted or blocked; the purchase checkbox records the user's separate review rather than granting entitlement. Reviewed blocked and apportioned purchase credit is implemented below. Initial holding and later temporary reversals/reclaims are described below; permanent later eligibility changes need additional workflows. [CBIC section 17](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000286/1000001), [CBIC input-credit rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/input-tax-credit-rules.html).

## Reviewed blocked and apportioned purchase ITC

Regular taxable purchases can record a reviewed eligible share of 0–100% for every line and a supporting basis. Zero is blocked/ineligible; an intermediate share is apportioned. This records the user's reviewed result, not an automatic section 17/rules 42–43 eligibility or apportionment determination. Supplier tax and the gross payable remain unchanged. [CBIC section 17](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000286/1000001).

Each input component rounds half up to paise after applying the line's eligible basis points. Only that amount debits the configured input-tax control. The remainder increases the line's expense or asset cost; inventory includes nonrecoverable tax in its carrying cost and subsequent weighted average/COGS. For a 1,000.00 purchase with 90.00 CGST and 90.00 SGST and a reviewed 50% share, debit expense/inventory 1,090.00, debit input CGST/SGST 45.00 each, and credit payable 1,180.00. With zero eligible share, the full 1,180.00 is cost. This follows the inventory-cost distinction between recoverable and nonrecoverable purchase taxes; other assets still require review of the correct capitalization policy. [ICAI AS 2, paragraph 7](https://indasaccess.icai.org/Volume-III/AS/asb.html?a=105).

A supplier return reverses original allocated credit and original nonrecoverable tax. Cumulative credit follows cumulative returned tax units, rather than independently rounded quantities, so a small note never reverses more credit than its tax. All notes together exhaust the original credit exactly. Purchase stock returns compare current carrying cost with original taxable credit plus its nonrecoverable tax; the variance remains explicit. Returns continue to require tax-adjustment review.

The optional `gst.itc_allocation` contains integer `eligible_bps` per line, a required `reason`, and optional reviewed `return_treatment`. `permanent_reversal` routes gross other ITC and the nonrecoverable portion to draft 4(A)(5) and 4(B)(1); `excluded_4d2` excludes that portion from 4(A)(5) and discloses it in 4(D)(2). The category must follow the reviewed legal reason; they are not interchangeable. [CBIC Circular 170/02/2022](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/Circular-170-02-2022-GST.pdf). Older periods before September 2022 and unmapped nonrecoverable allocations produce review items instead of guessed table-4 figures. Book recoverable tax is always shown and reconciled separately. GSTR-2B/IMS, applicable return rules and eligibility still need independent review.

Derived line `input_credit` values and allocation inputs require schema/backup version 6, with versions 1–5 readable. Restore recomputes costs, credit allocations and returns and rejects altered values or an older version label. Omitting the new fields preserves original fully reviewed purchase behavior and retry identity. Initial holding and temporary reversals/reclaims use the linked workflow below. Permanent later eligibility changes and automatic statutory apportionment calculations remain separate work.

## Pending ITC, temporary reversals and linked reclaims

A new regular taxable purchase can select **Hold reviewed recoverable ITC pending**, a dedicated asset account and the reviewed temporary-deferral basis. The purchase and a same-date temporary reversal are staged and saved together; a failed deferral leaves the destination purchase, journals and numbering unchanged. For the 1,000.00/180.00 synthetic example, expense/inventory remains 1,000.00, payable remains 1,180.00 and the full 180.00 is held in the pending asset, with zero net input-control debit. Blocked/nonrecoverable allocations still increase cost; only the separately reviewed recoverable portion can be held. This is a reviewed temporary treatment, not a determination of eligibility from receipt of an invoice.

For an existing purchase, open its details and use **Temporary ITC reversals & reclaims**. A temporary reversal debits a dedicated pending ITC asset and credits the relevant input controls. A later reclaim explicitly links that reversal, debits the same input components and credits its original pending account. Partial claims cannot exceed the remaining CGST, SGST/UTGST or IGST for that reversal. New deferrals cannot exceed the purchase's original recoverable components less amounts still pending. Payables, expense/inventory cost and stock quantities are unaffected by these transfers.

Each action requires an effective date, review basis and evidence declaration. Dates follow the purchase and prior ITC actions; closed financial periods reject new entries. Pending accounts cannot overlap GST controls, party/inventory mappings or unrelated postings. Posted adjustments and their source purchase cannot be reversed independently; corrective actions retain the linked history. The detail view exports current balances and the complete adjustment history, with paging after 50 records.

The register includes signed adjustment events on their effective dates. Draft periods from September 2022 map reviewed temporary reversals to 4(B)(2), linked reclaims to both 4(A)(5) and 4(D)(1), and show net mapped 4(C) as 4(A)(5) minus 4(B)(1) minus 4(B)(2). Other credit categories remain outside scope. Historical periods generate an unresolved mapping item. Review current GSTR-2B/IMS, electronic credit reversal/reclaim balances, interest, time limits and supporting evidence independently. This workflow does not calculate rule-37 deadlines/interest or establish portal entitlement. [CBIC Circular 170/02/2022, paragraph 4.3](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/Circular-170-02-2022-GST.pdf).

Supplier returns while a purchase has pending ITC are currently rejected. Do not reclaim unavailable credit to bypass this restriction: a return that clears a pending pool, permanent write-off/cost reclassification and retrospective eligibility changes need further workflows. Once all pending credit has been legitimately resolved, a dated supplier return can proceed; no further ITC actions may be added after a supplier return. Restore independently checks the event order, pools, source postings and dedication of pending accounts. Linked records require schema/backup version 7; versions 1–6 remain readable.

## Register and remaining scope

[GST return preparation](GST_RETURNS.md) now provides monthly/quarterly GSTR-1 candidate CSVs and GSTR-3B book worksheets, HSN review and tax-control reconciliation. These remain drafts; unresolved classifications, accounting corrections and rounding differences are explicit.

**Financial statements → GST accounting register** exports a dated, line-level review worksheet with HSN/SAC, rates, taxable values and separate tax components. Ordinary accounting reversals appear on their effective date with negative amounts. They are not represented as statutory credit/debit notes. Linked quantity returns now appear as negative `return_note` events on their note dates, with original-invoice references and cumulative proportional allocation of each tax component. Explicit adjustment-eligibility review is required. A buyer's purchase return note is a commercial record; review the supplier-issued statutory credit note separately. Notes include original invoice date/number, original particulars, return quantities and tax components in their printable export. Review all applicable Rule 53 particulars and section 34 eligibility/time limits independently. [CBIC Rule 53](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-rules/1000144/1000001), [CBIC section 34](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000304/1000001). The API explicitly returns `filing_ready: false`.

This is not a GSTR-1/GSTR-3B JSON uploader, portal filing integration, statutory note submission/eligibility determination, e-invoice/IRN/QR-code generator, or e-way bill system. Composition, reverse charge, SEZ/exports/deemed exports, mixed taxable/exempt documents, cess, advances, withholding, mixed/composite supply classification, multiple GST registrations per file, and time-of-supply/registration-date determinations are not implemented. Do not encode these cases as ordinary supported invoices.

GST Portal reporting contains additional tables and classifications beyond invoice tax arithmetic. The current portal instructions distinguish consumer invoice thresholds from older FAQ guidance and describe IRP-generated e-invoice metadata. No historical threshold or incomplete filing schema is embedded in the application. [Current official GSTR-1 preparation guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Creation_of_Outward_Supplies_Return_in_GSTR-1.htm).

Independent tax review and realistic anonymized fixtures remain release gates under issue #3. This specification was checked against the linked primary sources on 15 October 2026; changes to law, rates or portal formats require fresh verification.
